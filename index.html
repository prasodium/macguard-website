What it does

Everything runs on your Mac. When the charger is pulled while armed, these happen at once.

⚡

Charger detection

Watches the power source through macOS itself. Brief wiggles are filtered with a delay you choose, from immediate to 10 seconds.

🔔

Loud, repeating alarm

Pick the sound and repeat rate. It can raise the volume for you. Plug the charger back in and it stops, or stop it with Touch ID.

🔒

Screen lock

Asks macOS to lock the session and tells you honestly whether the lock was confirmed.

📍

Location in the alert

Uses Core Location with your permission. Every position is labelled fresh, cached or last known, with accuracy and time. Never faked.

✉️

Email to your Gmail

Sent straight through your own Brevo account to an address you verified with a 6-digit code. Includes a map link when coordinates are valid.

📶

Works offline-ish

No internet at the moment of theft? The alert is queued and sent when the connection returns, with careful retries.

🔁

Every unplug counts

Reconnect and unplug again, and it alarms again. Each time is its own incident with its own alert.

🧭

Find My guide

One button opens Apple's Find My with simple steps for Lost Mode. MacGuard never asks for your Apple password.

🪫

Light on battery

Event-driven, with a slow safety check. It lives in the menu bar and keeps protecting when the window is closed.

How it works

Set it up once, then arm it in one click whenever you leave your desk.

  1. Set up

    Add a free Brevo key and verify your Gmail in Settings. Takes about five minutes. Skip it and the alarm and lock still work.

  2. Plug in and arm

    MacGuard checks the charger is connected, shows you what it sees and asks you to confirm. It never arms silently.

  3. If the charger is pulled

    After your chosen delay: alarm, screen lock, location lookup and email, all started together. The alarm never waits for the email.

  4. Recover

    Follow the Find My steps in the email. Disarm with Touch ID or your Mac password when you are back.

Private by design

No MacGuard server exists. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to leak.

What stays on your Mac

  • Event log, locations and settings, in a folder only you can read
  • Your Brevo key, in the macOS Keychain
  • Passwords: never seen or stored. Touch ID and the system prompt do the checking

What leaves your Mac

  • Only the alert emails, through your own Brevo account to your verified address
  • No analytics, no telemetry, no IP-based guessing of location
  • Alerts never go to an address until you prove it is yours

What MacGuard can't do

An app on your Mac cannot stop a determined thief. It is meant to deter and to help you get your Mac back.

It cannot

  • Prevent shutdown, force-quit or removing all power
  • Sound the alarm while the Mac is asleep or off
  • Work before you log in
  • Promise the alarm is audible if speakers are muted
  • Show live tracking; location comes in occasional fixes

So pair it with

  • Find My Mac turned on, with the Find My network
  • A strong login password and FileVault
  • Lock Screen set to require a password immediately

Questions

The short answers. The docs have the rest.

Is it free?

Yes. MacGuard is open source. Brevo's free plan is enough for alerts and needs no domain, only one verified sender address. Check Brevo's site for current limits.

Does it work without the email setup?

Yes. The alarm, screen lock and event log work on their own. Email and location are optional extras.

Where does my Brevo key live?

In your Mac's Keychain, and nowhere else. If your Mac is stolen and not recovered, delete the key in Brevo.

Will it stop someone shutting down my Mac?

No. Nothing an app can do guarantees that, and MacGuard will not pretend otherwise. It raises the alarm and sends you the alert as fast as it can.

Why does it ask for Touch ID or my password?

To stop the alarm, disarm, quit while armed, or change email settings. The system prompt does the check, so MacGuard never sees your password.

Which Macs are supported?

macOS 13 or newer. It is built for Apple Silicon, tested first on a MacBook Air M2.

How do I remove it?

Run the uninstall script from the repository. It removes the app, its data and the Keychain item. Then delete the key in Brevo.
